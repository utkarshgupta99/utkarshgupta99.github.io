I am a security engineer interested in applying rigorous, theory-grounded methods to real-world deployments of information systems. My work involves (i) translating security objectives into precise system requirements, (ii) making the underlying assumptions explicit, and (iii) designing attacks to understand how such systems fail in practice. I am especially interested in designing reliable and trustworthy systems for security-critical aerospace and defense applications.

I am currently a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical Engineering at Northeastern University, working across AI security & privacy, cryptography, and secure communications. Before this, I earned a master’s degree in Electrical Engineering from the University of Michigan and an undergraduate degree in Mathematics and Computing from IIT Delhi. I have previously been a startup founder and CTO.

Outside of work, I enjoy boxing, playing the keyboard, and cooking.

Selected Works

Privacy in Federated Machine Learning over Multiple Training Rounds

Designed a user-selection protocol for secure aggregation that improves model convergence over the state of the art while guaranteeing privacy, aggregation fairness, and robustness to user dropouts.

Secure Wireless Communications Using Time-Varying Beampatterns

Proposed Secret-Shared Time-Modulated Array (SS-TMA), a novel phased-array architecture for physical-layer security that integrates secret sharing with time-modulated arrays for key management in resource-limited environments.

[IMS 2026]

Security of Secret-Sharing Schemes against Side-Channel Attacks

Improved bounds on the security guarantees of secret-sharing-based masking schemes, characterizing the trade-off between masking order and resilience to side-channel eavesdropping.

[EUROCRYPT 2026] [ISIT 2024]

Source-Inference Attacks

Designed a source-inference attack against a state-of-the-art shuffling-based federated-learning defense by adapting methods from membership-inference attacks, and validated its performance on models trained on imbalanced versions of MNIST and CIFAR.

Adversarial Attacks on Channel Estimation

Implemented gradient-based adversarial attacks against deep-learning models for wireless channel estimation, and proposed physically realizable over-the-air attacks under constraints relevant to wireless system security.
